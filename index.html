Watt

Watt, the puck.

A dial that becomes whatever you're doing.

  • On the desk, it's a volume knob.
  • Pick a face, it's a trackpad.
  • Open Claude, it listens.
  • In DialKit, it dials.
  • Claude Code asks? Tap to allow.
  • On a call? Flip it to mute.
  • Game night? Spin, teams, dice.
  • Head down? Focus.

Make one.

  1. 1  THE PUCK

    One board, no soldering

    Watt runs on the Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75C: a round touch screen in an aluminium case, with a battery, a speaker and a motion sensor.

    Plug it into your computer with a USB-C cable.

  2. 2  THE FIRMWARE

    Install it from here

    Click the button and pick the puck's port. It takes about a minute, and updates keep the puck paired.

    Open this page in Chrome or Edge Needs a secure (https) page

    Chrome or Edge on a computer. If the puck doesn't show up, hold BOOT, tap PWR, let go, and try again.

  3. 3  THE MAC APP

    Watt lives in the notch

    The app connects the puck to your Mac: volume, what's playing, the pointer, Claude, DialKit.

    Download for Mac

    macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon.

  4. 4  PAIR

    Dial it in

    Open Watt. When the puck drops out of the notch, click Pair, then turn the puck to each of the three numbers, like a safe.

    Click the puck's side button any time to pick a face.

In DialKit, it dials.

If you tune interfaces with DialKit, one line puts every knob on the puck: turn for the value, swipe for the next one, tap to flip a toggle.

It only follows the tab you're looking at, and only on your own machine.
